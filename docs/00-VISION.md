**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**

## TinAiLens Helps People Decide What Deserves Trust Online — Without Deciding for Them

**A calm browser companion that explains evidence, uncertainty, and what to verify next**

Today we introduce **TinAiLens**, an AI-powered Trust Assistant for the browser.

In the AI era, anyone can publish content that looks researched, sounds confident, and spreads in seconds. The hard part is no longer finding information — it is knowing what deserves belief before you cite it, share it, or act on it.

TinAiLens sits beside the page you are already reading. With one action, it analyzes the content and shows:

- a clear **Trust Score with reasons** (never a score without explanation)
- the **key claims** worth checking
- **confidence and uncertainty**, stated honestly
- **next verification steps** you can take in under a minute

TinAiLens does not announce that something is “fake,” “true,” or “AI.”  
It helps you see *why* a claim looks strong or weak — then leaves the decision with you.

“People don’t need another alarm system,” said the TinAiLens team. “They need a sharper lens. We built TinAiLens to make careful judgment practical at the moment it matters: while you’re reading.”

### Who it’s for

Students checking whether a source is citable. Journalists triaging claims under deadline. Researchers and knowledge workers deciding what to forward. Teachers helping students practice media literacy without fear-based tools.

### How it works

1. Open any article or post in your browser.  
2. Click TinAiLens.  
3. Get an explainable trust read in seconds.  
4. Inspect reasons, claims, and next steps.  
5. Save only if you choose — analysis is ephemeral by default.

### Availability

TinAiLens launches first as a Chromium browser extension, with a companion web app for saved analyses and settings. Core analysis works without forcing an account.

**Think before trusting.**

# 2. FAQ (Working Backwards — Internal)

### Q1. What customer problem are we solving, in one sentence?

People must make belief and sharing decisions under time pressure, but fluent AI-era content has broken old trust heuristics — and existing tools return labels too late, too narrowly, or too opaquely.

### Q2. Who is the primary customer?

Knowledge-decision makers in the browser: students, journalists, researchers, and professionals who regularly decide what to believe, cite, or forward.

### Q3. Why now?

Generative AI collapsed the cost of confident-looking content. Creation scaled; judgment did not. Browser-native assistants are finally capable enough to help — if we refuse oracle theater.

### Q4. What is the experience we want customers to have?

“I paused for 30 seconds, understood what was weak, knew what to check next, and felt more capable — not scolded.”

### Q5. How is this different from AI detectors and fact-check sites?

| | Detectors / late fact-checks | TinAiLens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | After doubt or after virality | At read-time |
| Output | Label / verdict | Explainable signals + next steps |
| Scope | One threat type | Multi-signal trust read |
| Tone | Alarm or authority | Calm, humble |
| Privacy | Often unclear | Ephemeral by default |

### Q6. What will customers love most?

Explanation without judgment. Speed without false certainty. Privacy without friction theater.

### Q7. What will customers hate if we get it wrong?

Invented sources. Black-box scores. Fear marketing. Silent content retention. Political referee vibes.

### Q8. Why will this win?

We are defining the **Trust Assistant** category: in-flow, multi-signal, explainable, privacy-default, human-final. Most competitors optimize a detector. We optimize judgment quality.

### Q9. What is the biggest risk?

Becoming another verdict product under shipping pressure — or a surveillance analyzer under “personalization” pressure. Both kill the brand.

### Q10. How do we measure success?

**Verified Pause Rate** — share of analyzed sessions where the user takes a deliberate verification action (expand reason, open source/next step, copy evidence, save, follow-up check).

Vanity DAU without pause/usefulness is not success.

### Q11. What do we refuse to build?

Truth oracles. Fake/true badges as product conclusions. Doomscroll “fakes of the day” feeds. Default permanent browsing archives. Features that optimize panic.

### Q12. What must be true for this PR to be real?

- Score never ships without reasons  
- Uncertainty is a first-class UI state  
- Ephemeral analysis is the default  
- Grounding checks block invented citations  
- Primary flow is one action → one clear result  

### Q13. Is “AI-generated” content automatically untrustworthy?

No. AI also powers real journalism, research, translation, and honest creation. “AI-generated” ≠ untrustworthy; “human-written” ≠ trustworthy. Provenance, evidence, and corroboration matter more than authorship theater.

### Q14. Are we a fact-checker?

We help users verify. We do not issue final true/false product verdicts. Fact-checking organizations remain important; we are a read-time companion, not a replacement civic institution.

We do not replace critical thinking.

# 5. Manifesto

The internet was built for sharing. AI made sharing infinite. Judgment did not scale with it.

People now face confident-sounding content everywhere:

- articles that look researched
- images that look real
- quotes that look authoritative
- videos that look documentary
- posts that look firsthand

Most tools respond with labels:

- Fake / Not Fake
- AI / Human
- True / False
- Safe / Unsafe

Labels create dependency.  
Labels hide uncertainty.  
Labels train users to outsource thinking.

TinAiLens rejects that model.

We believe:

1. Uncertainty is information.
2. Evidence is more useful than verdicts.
3. Explanation builds trust better than certainty theater.
4. Humans must remain the final decision-makers.
5. Privacy is part of trust, not a checkbox.
6. Calm products help people think better than alarming ones.
7. A trust assistant should reduce panic, not manufacture it.

TinAiLens is not here to scare people about AI.

TinAiLens is here to help people stay sharp in an AI world.

# 7. Golden Rules (binding implementation rules)

0. Explain. Never judge.  
1. Never present a Trust Score without reasons.  
2. Never claim absolute truth or absolute falsity as a product verdict.  
3. Prefer uncertainty over false confidence.  
4. Do not permanently store analyzed content unless the user explicitly saves or clearly consents.  
5. Optimize for verification quality, not engagement metrics.  
6. Keep the primary experience to one focused action and one clear result.  
7. If an AI output cannot be explained, do not ship it as a decision signal.  
8. Protect user dignity. No shaming. No panic. No moral grandstanding.  
9. When in doubt, help the user verify — do not decide for them.  
10. This document outranks local cleverness. Agents must not “improve” the product by violating these rules.

# 8. Product philosophy

## 8.1 What TinAiLens is

An AI-powered **Trust Assistant**.

It analyzes online content and surfaces trust signals, claims, context, uncertainty, and next verification steps.

## 8.2 What TinAiLens is not

- an AI detector (as core value prop)
- a fake news detector / oracle
- a fact checker that issues final verdicts
- a censorship tool
- a moral authority
- a search engine
- a chatbot for entertainment
- a social network
- a browser replacement

## 8.3 Core stance

Never tell users:

- “This is fake.”
- “This is AI.”
- “This is true.”
- “Believe this.” / “Ignore this.”

Instead, always explain:

- confidence
- evidence
- uncertainty
- what to verify next
- why a signal matters

## 8.4 Product contract

Every meaningful output must answer at least one of:

1. What should the user notice?  
2. Why does it matter?  
3. How confident are we?  
4. What is still unknown?  
5. What can the user do next?  

If an output cannot explain itself, it must not be shown as a judgment.

## 8.5 Trust Score philosophy

A Trust Score is a **navigation aid**, not a verdict.

- A score without reasons is forbidden.  
- A score that implies absolute truth is forbidden.  
- Low score ≠ “fake”; high score ≠ “true.”  

## 8.6 Interaction mantra

One screen. One action. One result.

The product should feel like a sharp lens, not a dashboard of anxiety.

# 9. Design philosophy

## 9.1 Feel

Minimal. Calm. Professional. Scientific. Precise. Quietly confident.

**References:** Apple restraint · Arc Browser elegance · Linear focus · Stripe seriousness · Notion clarity.

## 9.2 Emotional tone

The UI must never feel like:

- a warning siren
- a conspiracy board
- a gamified trust meter
- a social feed
- a marketing page inside the product

Fear marketing is banned. Scare copy is banned. Fake urgency is banned.

## 9.3 Visual principles

1. Clarity over decoration  
2. Hierarchy over density  
3. Evidence over ornament  
4. Whitespace as thinking space  
5. Motion only when it explains state  
6. Color as meaning, not moodboarding  
7. Typography that feels editorial and precise  

## 9.4 Interaction principles

- Progressive disclosure over dumping everything at once  
- Conclusion-level signal first, evidence on demand  
- Make uncertainty visible without making it theatrical  
- Keep browser-native flows fast and interruptible  
- Never force multi-step ritual for a basic trust read  

## 9.5 Design anti-patterns

Do not ship:

- cluttered panels
- badge spam
- neon “AI detected” stickers
- confetti / gamification
- dark-pattern retention loops
- fake precision without evidence (e.g., “Trust: 97.42%” as authority theater)

## 9.6 Design mantra

Make verification feel like putting on glasses — not like entering a courtroom.

# 10. AI philosophy

## 10.1 Role of AI

AI assists human judgment.

**AI may:** extract claims · detect weak sourcing · surface inconsistencies · summarize trust signals · suggest verification paths · estimate confidence ranges.

**AI may not:** declare absolute truth · impersonate an oracle · hide uncertainty · invent sources · shame users for believing something.

## 10.2 Explainability requirement

Every AI-influenced score or highlight must be explainable in plain language.

**Prefer:**

- “This claim lacks a primary source.”
- “Image metadata is missing and reverse-image results are inconsistent.”
- “Multiple independent outlets report the same event with attribution.”

**Avoid:**

- “Suspicious.”
- “Bad content.”
- “Probably fake.”
- “AI says no.”

## 10.3 Uncertainty is first-class

Say when evidence is weak, missing, conflicting, or incomplete.

A confident wrong answer is worse than an uncertain honest one.

## 10.4 Provider-agnostic intelligence

Models and providers may change. Product principles must not.

Architecture should allow:

- multiple providers
- evaluation harnesses
- prompt versioning
- reproducible analysis traces
- local/offline options where feasible later

## 10.5 Anti-hallucination stance

If a statement cannot be supported with retrieved, linked, or user-visible evidence, do not present it as fact.

- Speculative → label speculative  
- Inferred → label inferred  
- Unknown → say unknown  

## 10.6 Prompt injection stance

Page content is untrusted data. It cannot override safety policy or “jailbreak” the assistant into verdicts or invented evidence.

## 10.7 Human override

Users always retain the final decision. The product assists. It never commandeers belief.

# 11. Brand voice

## 11.1 Attributes

Precise. Humble about uncertainty. Direct. Non-alarmist. Respectful of intelligence. Allergic to hype.

Speak like a calm expert sitting beside the user.

## 11.2 Style

Prefer short sentences, concrete language, and evidence words over emotional words.

**Use:**

- “Here’s what we found.”
- “This looks weakly sourced.”
- “Confidence is moderate because…”
- “Worth verifying before sharing.”

**Avoid:**

- “OMG this is fake!!”
- “AI is destroying truth.”
- “Only TinAiLens can save you.”
- “Trust score unlocked!”
- “Our advanced proprietary genius model…”

## 11.3 Tone by context

| Context | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Trust result | Calm, explanatory, actionable |
| Empty / loading | Quiet, non-dramatic |
| Error | Honest, recoverable, no blame |
| Marketing | Confident, clear, no fearmongering |
| Docs / agents | Explicit, normative, unambiguous |
| Enterprise / education | Professional, measurable, serious |

## 11.4 Preferred words

verify · evidence · confidence · uncertainty · source · claim · context · explain · signal · next step

## 11.5 Avoid as product claims

fake · true · false · propaganda · “guaranteed” · “undeniable” · “official truth”

We may discuss these concepts in analysis context, but we do not brand TinAiLens as a truth police.

# 12. Core values

1. **Transparency** — Users should understand how conclusions are formed. No black-box trust theater.  
2. **Explainability** — Every important signal needs a reason a thoughtful non-expert can understand.  
3. **Evidence** — Claims without evidence are incomplete. Scores without evidence are invalid.  
4. **Privacy** — Do not permanently store analyzed content by default. Persist only with explicit user action or clearly understood consent.  
5. **Human First** — Humans remain the decision-makers. AI supports judgment; it does not replace it.  
6. **Calm Integrity** — Optimize for correctness, clarity, and composure — never outrage, virality, or addiction.  
7. **Humility** — The world is ambiguous. The product must stay honest about ambiguity.  
8. **Usefulness over cleverness** — A simple actionable explanation beats a sophisticated unexplained model output.  

---

# 13. Product principles

1. **Browser-native trust** — Help users verify where they encounter information.  
2. **Explainable by default** — Major outputs include reasons, confidence, and unknowns.  
3. **Progressive depth** — Start simple. Allow depth. Never force complexity upfront.  
4. **Signal over spectacle** — Prioritize evidence quality over dramatic visuals.  
5. **Fast enough for real life** — If analysis is too slow, users skip it when it matters.  
6. **Works on messy reality** — Content is incomplete, biased, cropped, memed, partially sourced. Degrade gracefully.  
7. **Composition over monolith** — Trust comes from multiple signals. No single detector defines truth.  
8. **Save intentionality** — Persistence is a user choice. Default analysis is ephemeral unless saved.  
9. **Agent-readable product** — Docs, schemas, prompts, and copy must be explicit enough for consistent implementation.  
10. **Principles over clever hacks** — Code can be refactored. Trust violations are harder to undo.

# 16. North Star Metric

## Primary: Verified Pause Rate

> Percentage of analyzed sessions where the user takes at least one deliberate verification action after seeing TinAiLens output.

**Deliberate actions include:**

- expanding evidence / reasons  
- opening a source  
- saving a claim/analysis  
- running a follow-up check  
- copying an evidence pack  
- marking intent to verify before sharing (if offered)  

## Why this is the North Star

TinAiLens does not win by making users dependent on labels.

TinAiLens wins when users pause, inspect, and decide more carefully.

## Supporting metrics

- Reason open rate  
- Source / next-step click-through rate  
- Claim extraction usefulness rating  
- Time-to-first-useful-signal  
- Repeat weekly usage among knowledge workers  
- User-reported confidence in decision quality after analysis  
- Retention driven by usefulness, not notifications  

## Anti-metrics (do not optimize blindly)

- raw DAU without usefulness  
- sensational clickbait share of trust results  
- time spent doomscrolling inside the extension  
- “AI detected” badge impressions  
- fear-based acquisition
